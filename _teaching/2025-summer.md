## Course Description
A rigorous treatment of multivariable analysis with emphasis on differentiability and integration in higher dimensions. Topics include metric spaces, continuity and compactness, differentiability in $\mathbb{R}^n$, Jacobians, $k$–volume via Gram determinants, change of variables, Fubini/Tonelli, and integral theorems (Green, Stokes, Divergence) with applications to data science, machine learning, and physics.

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the course, students will be able to:
1. **Topology of metric spaces:** work with limits, Cauchy sequences, completeness, compactness, and connectedness; prove standard results.
2. **Differentiability in $\mathbb{R}^n$:** compute and reason with Jacobians; apply chain, inverse, and implicit function theorems.
3. **$k$–volume & Gram determinants:** compute volumes of $k$–parallelepipeds; use Pythagorean identities for $k$–volume.
4. **Measure theory:** understand the construction of Riemann integration; Lebesgue measure, general measure space; motivation and construction of Lebesgue integration.
5. **Integration theory:** state and apply Fubini/Tonelli; perform multivariable change of variables with $C^1$–diffeomorphisms.
6. **Integral theorems:** state and apply Green’s, Stokes’, and the Divergence theorems; interpret flux/circulation and orientation.
7. **Communication:** write clear, self-contained solutions with precise definitions and correct citations of theorems.

## Prerequisites / Expected Background
- Prior courses in single-variable analysis and linear algebra (or equivalent).  
- Comfort with $\varepsilon$–$\delta$ proofs and matrix calculus.

## How to Succeed
- **Practice steadily.** Start problem sets early; work beyond the assigned minimum.  
- **Prove carefully.** State definitions/theorems you use; justify each step.  
- **Check hypotheses.** For results like Fubini/CoV/IFT, verify assumptions (e.g., $C^1$–diffeomorphism, absolute integrability).  
- **Reflect.** After feedback, correct mistakes and summarize take-aways.
